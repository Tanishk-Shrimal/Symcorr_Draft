\subsection{Motivation}

Lattice QCD provides a non-perturbative framework for studying the properties
and interactions of hadrons directly from quantum chromodynamics. In hadron
spectroscopy, the quantities of interest are commonly extracted from Euclidean
correlation functions constructed using interpolating operators with the
quantum numbers of the states being studied.

Modern spectroscopy calculations often require a basis containing several
interpolating operators rather than a single operator. Different operators may
contain different spin structures, momenta, spatial constructions, or
combinations of hadrons. Correlation matrices constructed from such operator
bases provide the information required to resolve the finite-volume energy
spectrum and to distinguish states with similar quantum numbers.

As the number and complexity of the interpolating operators increase,
constructing the corresponding correlation functions becomes increasingly
cumbersome. For every combination of source and sink operators, the allowed
Wick contractions must be determined, fermionic signs must be treated
consistently, and the flavor, spin, color, and spatial structures must be
carried through to the numerical calculation. Although many of these steps are
systematic, performing them manually for every new operator basis requires
substantial implementation effort and can introduce bookkeeping errors.

The distillation framework~\cite{Peardon:2009gh} provides a particularly useful
setting for spectroscopy calculations because it separates quark propagation
from the operator-dependent structures used to construct hadrons. This makes
it possible to construct a wide range of interpolating operators from a common
set of quark-propagation data. Nevertheless, the transformation from the
mathematical definition of an interpolating operator to the complete numerical
expression for its correlation function remains a non-trivial task.

\textsc{SymCorr} is developed to automate this transformation. Interpolating
operators are first expressed symbolically, after which the package determines
the corresponding Wick contractions and systematically transforms the
resulting expressions toward their numerical representation in the
distillation framework. The aim is to reduce the amount of correlator-specific
algebra and implementation that must be performed manually, while keeping the
physical definition of an operator separate from the details of its numerical
evaluation.

\subsection{Challenges in lattice QCD correlator construction}

For a simple interpolating operator, constructing a two-point correlation
function can be relatively straightforward. A meson operator, for example,
contains one quark and one antiquark field, and the corresponding Wick
contractions involve only a small number of quark lines. The structure becomes
more involved when the operators contain several quark fields or when many
operators are combined into a correlation matrix.

For a basis of interpolating operators \(\{\mathcal O_i\}\), one evaluates the
matrix of correlation functions

\begin{equation}
C_{ij}(t)
=
\left\langle
\mathcal O_i(t)\,\mathcal O_j^{\dagger}(0)
\right\rangle .
\end{equation}

Each source--sink pair can contain different flavor, spin, momentum, and
spatial structures. These structures must be retained consistently while all
allowed contractions between quark and antiquark fields are identified. When
several fields of the same flavor are present, there can be multiple valid
pairings, and the relative signs associated with fermionic permutations must
also be handled correctly.

The amount of bookkeeping therefore grows as one moves from simple meson
operators to baryons and multi-hadron operators. At the same time, realistic
spectroscopy calculations require many source--sink combinations, so the same
basic contraction patterns and operator-dependent structures can appear
repeatedly across different matrix elements.

After the Wick contractions have been determined, the resulting propagator
expressions must still be translated into the objects used for numerical
evaluation. In the distillation framework this means expressing the correlator
in terms of perambulators and operator-dependent structures in the Laplacian
eigenvector space. Carrying out this translation separately for each operator
combination makes the implementation difficult to extend and increases the
amount of repeated algebra that must be checked by hand.

These difficulties motivate a systematic representation of the operators and
their contractions. In \textsc{SymCorr}, the operator definition, Wick
contraction, and subsequent translation to the distillation representation are
treated as distinct stages. This allows each stage to be constructed and
verified independently before introducing the numerical optimizations required
for large correlation matrices.

\subsection{Related software and prior approaches}

Several tools address different parts of the operator-construction and
correlator-evaluation workflow in lattice QCD. The Multi-Hadron Interpolators
(MHI) package implements a general procedure for combining operators with
definite momenta and projecting them into irreducible representations of the
appropriate lattice little groups~\cite{PhysRevD.109.094516}. More recently,
\texttt{OpTion} provides a systematic construction of multi-hadron lattice
operators with general momentum, spin, and internal quantum numbers, with
explicit constructions for systems containing up to four hadrons
~\cite{Yan:2025op}. These approaches address the important problem of building
interpolating operators with the required finite-volume symmetry properties.

A separate class of work focuses on the subsequent evaluation of correlation
functions. The Redstar analysis framework represents Wick-contraction terms as
graphs and uses graph-contraction algorithms to reduce redundant work and to
organize tensor contractions for execution on CPUs and GPUs
~\cite{10.1145/3592979.3593409}. Its formulation includes tensors carrying
both distillation and spin indices, and therefore provides an important example
of optimized correlator evaluation in a distillation-based calculation.
Related algorithmic work has also explored determinant representations of Wick
contractions and methods for identifying smaller sets of determinant terms that
are sufficient for evaluating nuclear correlation functions
~\cite{PhysRevD.110.114505}.

These developments address complementary parts of a broad computational
problem. The emphasis of \textsc{SymCorr} in the present work is on treating
the complete transformation from a symbolic quark-level operator description
to a numerical distillation calculation as a sequence of explicit compilation
stages. Operator representation, Wick contraction, translation to
backend-independent physics objects, distillation lowering, execution planning,
and numerical evaluation are kept as separate layers of the software. This
staged organization is intended to make the algebraic transformations easier to
inspect and validate while allowing numerical execution strategies to evolve
without changing the original physics description of the interpolating
operators.

\subsection{Scope and contributions of SymCorr}

\textsc{SymCorr} is intended to operate on the part of a distillation-based
calculation that begins once the required lattice data are available. In
particular, it contracts precomputed numerical objects such as Laplacian
eigenvectors and perambulators; it does not generate the Laplacian eigenvectors
or perform inversions of the Dirac operator. Its role is to connect the
high-level specification of interpolating operators to the construction and
numerical evaluation of the corresponding correlation functions.

The main contributions of the present implementation are:

\begin{itemize}

\item A symbolic representation of lattice-QCD interpolating operators together
with automatic Wick contraction, so that the quark-field content and allowed
fermionic contractions can be handled before numerical storage or execution
details are introduced.

\item A staged compilation pipeline in which the contracted expressions are
first translated into a backend-independent physics representation and are then
lowered to the quantities required by the distillation formalism. This keeps
the physics-level transformations separate from the later numerical execution
choices.

\item A global representation of the numerical contraction problem that makes
repeated structures visible across the calculation. The current implementation
uses this representation for contraction-order selection, reuse of common
intermediate quantities, scheduling, and memory planning before numerical
execution.

\item A numerical runtime that binds the compiled calculation to externally
supplied lattice data and evaluates the resulting contractions on CPU or GPU.
The execution layer includes automatic hardware and memory checks, while the
symbolic physics description remains independent of the machine on which the
calculation is run.

\item A validation strategy that separates algebraic and compiler regression
tests from comparison with independent lattice-QCD data. At present, the
external-data validation envelope includes rest- and moving-frame single-meson
correlators, several bilocal two-hadron correlators, and a tested local
four-quark contraction with a known external normalization difference. Other
implemented pathways are not assumed to have the same level of numerical
validation unless stated explicitly later in this work.

\end{itemize}

This distinction between implemented functionality and externally validated
physics calculations is maintained throughout the paper. Performance claims
are likewise deferred to controlled benchmarks presented separately, rather
than being inferred from the existence of individual optimization stages.
